# XX — West with the Night

### *Beryl Markham*

**"I learned what every dreaming child needs to know — that no horizon is so far that you cannot get above it or beyond it."** — *Beryl Markham*

### **[ The Author: Beryl Markham ]**

Beryl Markham lived a life that would make most adventurers look lazy. She moved to Kenya at age three and was raised by her father and Kipsigis tribesmen. She spent her childhood hunting wild game with spears. She spoke Swahili before she spoke proper English.

She was a woman who did not care for rules. She became the first female licensed racehorse trainer in Africa. She later became a bush pilot scouting elephants from the air. Her greatest feat was flying solo across the Atlantic from east to west in 1936. This was much harder than what Lindbergh did because she flew against the prevailing winds.

She crashed in a swamp in Nova Scotia but she survived. Ernest Hemingway once said her writing made him feel like a mere carpenter with words. She died in 1983 just as her book was being rediscovered by the world.

---

### **[ The Book ]**

This is not a standard autobiography. It is a collection of memories that feel like a vivid dream. The prose is beautiful and sharp. Markham writes about her life in a Kenya that no longer exists. She talks about the smell of the earth and the heat of the sun. She weaves together three different lives. She is a hunter. She is a horse trainer. She is a pilot.

The writing is dramatic and full of life. She describes the thrill of the hunt and the bond between a rider and a horse. She captures the feeling of being the only person in the sky.

The most famous part of the book is her flight across the Atlantic. She describes the sheer terror of being alone over the dark water for twenty hours. Her fuel lines froze and her engine died. She crashed in the mud but walked away.

The book disappeared during World War II and was forgotten for decades. It was found again in the 1980s and became a massive hit. It is a masterpiece because it captures the spirit of a woman who refused to be ordinary. She shows us that the horizon is only a limit if you let it be. It is a story about courage and the deep need for solitude.

### **[ Destinations Explored ]**

* **Njoro, Kenya:** The Rift Valley farm where young Beryl learned to hunt with Kipsigis warriors and speak Swahili.
* **East African Skies:** Unmapped territory flown by instinct where elephants appear as gray shadows below.
* **Atlantic Ocean:** Twenty hours of darkness and ice between England and Nova Scotia over "all that black water."
* **Nairobi Racetracks:** Where she trained champions and proved a woman could succeed in a male dominated field.

### **[ Further Reading ]**

**About Beryl Markham:**

* ***Straight on Till Morning*** by Mary S. Lovell: The definitive biography that explores her life beyond the memoir.
* ***Circling the Sun*** by Paula McLain: A novel that fictionalizes Markham's early years and her complex relationships.

**In a Similar Vein:**

* ***Out of Africa*** by Karen Blixen: The other classic Kenya memoir from the same era and landscape.
