# XX — A Short Walk in the Hindu Kush

### *Eric Newby*

**"I was a traveler of a very low order."** — *Eric Newby*

---

### **[ The Essentials ]**

* **Genre:** Travel Memoir
* **Sub-Genre:** Adventure, Humorous Travel
* **Keywords:** *Afghanistan, Nuristan, Mountaineering, Panjshir Valley, Amateurism, British Humor*
* **Traveler Profile:** **The Accidental Adventurer.** You love a story where enthusiasm outweighs expertise.

---

### **[ The Author: Eric Newby ]**

Eric Newby was a man trapped in the wrong world. He spent his days in the London fashion industry working for a firm called Worth. He found the luxury of the city stifling and dull. In 1956 he sent a short telegram to his friend Hugh Carless. He asked if Hugh was free to travel to Afghanistan. This was the birth of a legend.

Newby was not a professional explorer. He was a man who preferred a good laugh to a stiff upper lip. He took a four-day crash course in Wales to learn how to climb. That was the only training he had. He became one of the most famous travel writers of the century. He wrote with a dry wit and a total lack of vanity.

---

### **[ The Book ]**

The journey is a wild trek through the Panjshir Valley toward the frozen peak of Mir Samir. This mountain towers over the world at 19,000 feet. Newby and Hugh were hopelessly out of their depth. They staggered through the mountains with stomachs destroyed by dysentery and feet torn into raw meat. The writing is sharp and fast and full of life. It celebrates the grit of the road.

Newby does not pretend to be a hero. He records every stumble and every mistake with a wicked sense of humor. He brings the rugged beauty of Afghanistan to the page with vivid and dramatic detail. The final scene is the most famous in travel literature. They meet the legendary explorer Wilfred Thesiger in a remote camp.

Thesiger lived a life of brutal asceticism. He watched in horror as Newby and Hugh inflated their air mattresses for the night. He called them a pair of pansies. This encounter is the soul of the book. It pits the old world of the explorer against the new world of the traveler. It is a masterpiece because it finds the humor in hardship.

---

### **[ Destinations Explored ]**

* **Kabul:** The chaotic and dusty starting point where the two friends began their Afghan expedition.
* **The Panjshir Valley:** A stunningly beautiful valley that led them deep into the heart of the mountains.
* **Mir Samir:** The daunting mountain peak that tested their physical limits and their fragile climbing skills.
* **Nuristan:** A remote and hidden province where ancient traditions and mountain secrets held sway.

---

### **[ Further Reading ]**

**By Newby:**

* ***Slowly Down the Ganges***: A funny and insightful account of a 1,200-mile journey down India's most holy river.
* ***Love and War in the Apennines***: A moving memoir of his time spent as a prisoner of war in the mountains of Italy.

**In a Similar Vein:**

* ***Arabian Sands*** by Wilfred Thesiger: The stark and very serious flip side to Newby's amateur approach.
* ***The Road to Oxiana*** by Robert Byron: A classic account of travel through the same rugged region of the world.
