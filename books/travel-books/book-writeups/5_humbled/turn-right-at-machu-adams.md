# XX — Turn Right at Machu Picchu

### *Mark Adams*

**"I was an adventure-magazine editor who had never spent a night in a tent."** — *Mark Adams*

---

### **[ The Essentials ]**

* **Genre:** Travelogue
* **Sub-Genre:** Investigative History
* **Keywords:** *Peru, Andes, Machu Picchu, Hiram Bingham, Inca, Archaeology, Trekking, Adventure*
* **Traveler Profile:** **The Accidental Explorer.** You like history lessons that come with sore muscles and a good laugh.

---

### **[ The Author: Mark Adams ]**

Mark Adams was a man of the indoors. He spent his career as an editor for adventure magazines in New York. He sat in a comfortable chair and read about people doing dangerous things. He never actually did them himself. Then he turned forty. He decided to leave the office behind to solve a century old mystery. He wanted to know the truth about Hiram Bingham. Bingham was the Yale professor who found Machu Picchu in 1911. Some called him a hero. Others called him a grave robber. Adams hired a legendary guide named John Leivers to find out. Leivers was a tough Australian who lived in the mountains. He pushed Adams to his physical limits. Adams writes with a sharp and self deprecating wit. He transforms from a soft city dweller into a man who can handle the brutal Andean trails.

---

### **[ The Book ]**

The book is a dual narrative that bridges two different eras. We see Hiram Bingham hacking through the thick Peruvian jungle a hundred years ago. We also see Adams following those same muddy tracks today. He visits remote ruins like Choquequirao that most tourists never see. The writing is conversational and full of colorful details. He describes the taste of coca tea and the bite of the mountain cold. He dives deep into the history of the Inca Empire. He explains how they carved massive stones to fit together without any mortar.

This is more than a simple hiking story. It is a detective tale about the discovery of a lost world. Adams looks at the legal battles over the artifacts Bingham took back to America. He questions who has the right to own history. The climax comes when they finally reach the city in the clouds. The description of the mist clearing at Machu Picchu is dramatic and powerful. It shows the site as a living masterpiece rather than just a photo op. This book belongs in the top 100 because it makes complex history feel like a fast paced adventure. It is a perfect guide for anyone who wants to see the real Peru beyond the postcards.

---

### **[ Destinations Explored ]**

* **Cusco:** The ancient Incan capital where the journey begins and the altitude starts to bite.
* **Choquequirao:** A massive and remote ruin that is far harder to reach than its famous neighbor.
* **The Vilcabamba:** The rugged region where the last Incan emperors held out against the Spanish.
* **Machu Picchu:** The spectacular city in the clouds that remains the crown jewel of the Andes.
