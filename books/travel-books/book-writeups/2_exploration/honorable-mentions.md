# Honorable Mentions: II. Exploration at the Edge

_9 additional books that belong in this category_

---

**A Traveller on Horseback**, Christina Dodwell (Middle East) — A woman rides solo through Turkey and Iran in the 1980s, navigating mountains, hospitality, and a male-dominated landscape on horseback.

**Brazilian Adventure**, Peter Fleming (South America) — Peter Fleming's comic misadventure searching for a lost explorer in the Amazon, proving that not all expeditions need succeed to entertain.

**Eiger Dreams**, Jon Krakauer (Multiple) — Jon Krakauer's early essays on obsession and death in the mountains, profiling climbers who risk everything for one more summit.

**Into Thin Air**, Jon Krakauer (Asia / Himalayas) — The deadliest day on Everest: a journalist's harrowing firsthand account of the 1996 disaster that killed eight climbers in a single storm.

**Kon-Tiki**, Thor Heyerdahl (Pacific Ocean) — Norwegian explorer crosses 5,000 miles of Pacific on a balsa raft to prove Polynesia could have been settled from South America.

**Lost in Shangri-La**, Mitchell Zuckoff (Oceania / New Guinea) — A World War II plane crash in New Guinea drops survivors into a Stone Age valley untouched by the modern world.

**Running the Amazon**, Joe Kane (South America) — The first person to walk the Amazon's entire length, from Peruvian glaciers to the Atlantic—860 days through jungle, mountains, and madness.

**Stranger in the Forest**, Eric Hansen (Asia / Borneo) — Walking across Borneo's interior through headhunter territory, relying on rainforest tribes for food, shelter, and safe passage through the wild.

**Tiger of the Snows**, Tenzing Norgay (Asia / Himalayas) — Tenzing Norgay's own story of conquering Everest, told from the Sherpa perspective often overshadowed by Edmund Hillary's fame.
